What happens on your computer

The commands you send from your phone run on your computer as if you used its own mouse and keyboard.

  • Locking, sleeping, restarting and shutting down ask you to confirm, but restarting or shutting down may close programs with unsaved work. Save your work first.
  • A tap, gesture or shortcut may reach the wrong window if another one is in front. Check what is on your screen before sending important commands.
  • If you edit a presentation's notes from your phone, the change is saved in your file. Keep a copy of your important presentations.
  • The Windows program opens when you sign in and, when installed, creates a Windows Firewall permission so your phone can find it. You can turn off automatic start from its icon and remove everything by uninstalling it.

Your network and your security

Pocket PC communicates with your computer over your local network, a cable or Bluetooth. That communication is not end-to-end encrypted.

  • Use it on trusted networks, such as your home or office network. On a public network, other people could try to see or interfere with that communication.
  • Authorize only your own phones. If you lose an authorized phone or no longer use it, delete the %APPDATA%\PocketPC folder on your PC: all phones will then have to ask for permission again.
  • Keep your phone, your computer and your antivirus up to date. The security of your devices and your network is your responsibility.
